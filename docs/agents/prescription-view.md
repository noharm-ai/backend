# Prescription View, Interactions and Allergy

## Prescription View (`GET /prescriptions/<id>`)
- The most used endpoint (`prescription_view_service._internal_get_prescription`); prescalc runs the same pipeline (`static_get_prescription`), so a cost added here is paid on every load of the screen and on every prescalc
- **What was read once is passed along, not read again**: the prescription's segment (joined in `_get_prescription_data`, passed to `_get_configs`), the tenant features (read with the other memory items, kept for the request by `feature_service.set_features`), the feature flags (one read per request, also when none is set), the patient's allergy records (alerts, notes and cross reactivity) and the columns the models defer but the view shows (`undefer` in `_get_prescription_data`). `tests/integration/test_prescription_view_queries.py` fails when one of these becomes a query of its own again
- A new read on this path: join it to a query that already runs or reuse what was read; a check per item or per pair of items must not query, nor look things up in a list (the interaction check compares every pair of items)

## Converted Dose (dose in the drug's default unit)
- **The converted dose is computed live, never read from `presmed.doseconv`**: the dose shown on the screen times the factor of the prescribed unit (`prescriptionutils.get_converted_dose`, `DrugList.get_converted_dose` for a view row). Every reader of a dose in the default unit uses it: the dose per weight ("ou X mcg/Kg", also in the infection control timeline), the max dose and vancomycin IRA alerts (`alert_service._get_dose_conv`), the solution calculator (`DrugList.get_solution_dose`), the carboplatin AUC (`DrugList.get_auc_value`, prescribed in another unit than mg) and the dose popover (`convertedDose` on each drug list item). So an alert always matches the dose the pharmacist sees
- The factor comes from `unidadeconverte` (`measure_unit_convert_factor`), joined on `presmed.fkunidademedida` (not on the `unidademedida` row, which a unit may lack). Prescribed in the default unit it needs none (`prescriptionutils.is_same_unit`: the same id, or the same `unidademedida_nh` under two ids, e.g. "MILIGRAMA" and "mg"; both names come with the view's query, and the infection control timeline joins the default unit's `unidademedida` in its own. Like the prescribed unit, the default unit is joined on the lowest `fkhospital` that registers it, not on the prescription's hospital: a unit may be registered for another hospital only)
- Without a factor for another unit it can't be told (None), and each reader decides: no "ou ..." in the dose per weight, the alerts take the dose as already in the default unit (factor 1), the solution calculator counts 0 and disables the total, the AUC is missing (`dose`), the popover says so (`missingConvertFactor`, below)
- **`presmed.doseconv` is prescalc's outlier score key** (`complete_presmed_v2`), not a dose: for a drug with dose ranges (`medatributos.divisor`) it is rounded up to the range and divided by the weight found, and a re-sent item gets a new one from the data sent again while most schemas' presmed triggers (`ON CONFLICT DO UPDATE`) keep the `dose` and unit of the first insert (on the replica, 2026-11-17, 2% of the recent items without ranges in the ten schemas with the most such items; 86% of those re-sent). It is read as a dose only without one (dose diferenciada: `doseconv` is the daily total), except for a range drug. Comparisons of stored values keep reading it: the check history (`checkedindex`), the infection control posology change, the outliers
- The max dose alerts drop the float noise where the dose is computed, not only in the text: each item's daily dose (`_get_dose_conv`), each sum (`_get_dose_total`) and the dose per Kg, single and summed, are rounded to 7 significant digits (`numberutils.round_significant`, what a float4 holds), so 0.1 × 3 doesn't exceed a max dose of 0.3 and the dose compared is the dose printed. Never to 2 decimals: 0.2 mg for 50 Kg is 0.004 mg/Kg, which 2 decimals turn into 0, and a max dose of 0.003 mg/Kg would never alert
- Their texts (single and summed, per Kg or not) print the dose and the max dose with `stringutils.format_number_br`: Brazilian format, 2 decimals from 1 up, and below 1 the decimals 3 significant digits need ("1.522,62 mg", "33,33 mg", "0,375 mg" where `strFormatBR` gives "0,38")
- `missingConvertFactor` on each drug list item (`prescriptionutils.is_convert_factor_missing`): the drug has a default unit, the item is prescribed in another one (not the same `unidademedida_nh`) and there is no factor. The screening marks the dose with it, as it marks a drug without substance
- Tests: `tests/unit/test_drug_list_dose_weight.py`, `tests/unit/test_alert_service_dose.py`, `tests/unit/test_drug_list_infusion.py`, `tests/unit/test_drug_list_auc.py`

## Drug Interaction Checking
- Service: `services/alert_interaction_service.py`
- Checks drug-drug interactions
- Multiple severity levels
- Every pair of items is compared (the work grows with the square of the items), so the pairs are deduplicated in a dict and the pairs without a relation are skipped with one lookup. The relations of the substance pairs are read in one statement that is the same for every prescription: the pairs go as two array parameters (`_get_active_relations`), not written into the SQL

## Allergy Alert
- Rule: `alert_service.is_allergic`, used by the allergy alert and by the item's `allergy` flag in the drug list (`utils/drug_list.py`)
- The flag on `presmed.alergia` is set when the item arrives; the prescription view confirms it with the patient's `alergia` records (`repository/allergy_repository.py`): an active record of the item raises the alert, only inactive ones drop it, and with no record of the item the flag stands (some clients have no `alergia` records)
- A record is of the item when its `fkmedicamento` is the same drug (even if its `sctid` is stale), or by substance: `alergia.sctid` when present (filled by a separate job and trusted, since `fkmedicamento` does not always reference a `medicamento` record), otherwise the substance of its `fkmedicamento`. The cross reactivity check (`alert_interaction_service._get_allergies`) resolves the substance from the same records (`get_allergy_records` joins it that way), which the prescription view passes to it
- The prescription view reads the records once (`_internal_get_prescription`) and passes them to the alerts, to the cross reactivity and to the clinical notes, which list the active ones (source `pep`, one per name, cut at `_ALLERGY_RECORDS_LIMIT`: only information)
- **A substance tagged `no_allergy_alert`** (`SubstanceTagEnum.NO_ALLERGY_ALERT`, given by the curators in admin; `alert_service.is_allergy_alert_disabled`) never raises an allergy, for every client: `is_allergic` answers no whatever the flag and the records say, even an active record. It is the item's substance, the `Substance` the view's query already joins (`item[11]`), so it costs no query; an item whose drug has no substance can't be tagged. The cross reactivity leaves it out on both sides: a tagged prescribed item is not compared with the allergies (it still is with the other items), and an allergy record whose substance is tagged is not one of the patient's allergies (`_get_allergies`, from `substanceTags` in `get_allergy_records`; so the interaction trace does not list it). The interaction trace explains a tagged item with the check `allergyAlertDisabled`
- Each drug list item carries why, for the maintainers (drug popover in the frontend): `allergyFlag` (`presmed.alergia` as it arrived), `allergyRecord` (`active`/`inactive` from `alert_service.get_allergy_record_status`, `None` when no record of the item decided and the flag stood; still filled for a tagged substance) and `allergyAlertDisabled` (the substance is tagged `no_allergy_alert`)
- Tests: `tests/unit/test_alerts_allergy.py` and `tests/integration/test_prescription_allergy.py`
